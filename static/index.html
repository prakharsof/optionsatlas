What is implied volatility?

Implied volatility (IV) is the volatility level that makes an option pricing model match the option’s market price. Higher IV often means options cost more. It describes the market’s priced uncertainty, not whether an asset will rise or fall.

New to options?Our explains the terms as you explore.
Implied volatility (IV)

How much movement option prices imply over time, annualized. IV does not indicate direction.

Delta

Approximate option-price change for a $1 move in the underlying. Calls are usually positive; puts negative.

Gamma

How quickly delta changes as the underlying price moves.

Theta

Estimated daily time decay, all else equal. Usually negative for long options.

Vega

Estimated change in option value for a one percentage point change in implied volatility.

Open interest

Outstanding contracts not yet closed or exercised. Volume counts contracts traded during the session.

Volatility skew

Differences in IV across strikes. Puts may carry higher IV as investors price downside protection.

Unusual activity

High trading volume relative to open interest can be notable, but doesn’t reveal the strategy or trader’s intent.
